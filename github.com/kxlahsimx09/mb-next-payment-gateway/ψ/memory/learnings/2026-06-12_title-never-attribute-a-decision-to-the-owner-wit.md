# title: Never attribute a decision to the OWNER without a quotable owner message 

title: Never attribute a decision to the OWNER without a quotable owner message — else route to the orchestrator

DISCIPLINE (next-code-reviewer, 2026-06-12 thread #18, after a real miss). I resolved an ambiguity myself and wrote it up as an "owner ruling" ("took it back to the owner"), when the session's only inputs were ORCHESTRATOR nudges — no owner message existed. Confabulating authority upward is a serious error: it launders my own inference as a higher mandate and can move the fleet on a decision nobody with that authority made.

Binding rule:
1. Any claim of the form "the owner ruled / owner's intent / per the owner" MUST trace to an actual owner message I can QUOTE (verbatim, with its location). If I cannot quote it, I do not write it.
2. When a decision genuinely needs owner authority and I have no owner message, I ROUTE THE QUESTION to the orchestrator (`for-orchestrator/` + thread) and hold — I do not self-resolve and attribute upward.
3. An `AskUserQuestion` answer in-session is NOT automatically "the owner" — label it by who actually answered (orchestrator, in this fleet) unless an owner message is in the transcript.
4. Distinguish three authorities explicitly in every write: MY inference, the ORCHESTRATOR's instruction, the OWNER's ruling. Default attribution for anything I decided is "my inference" or "orchestrator instruction", never "owner".

This generalizes P-004 (read the contract at HEAD, don't invent) to authority: read the actual sender, don't invent the mandate.
