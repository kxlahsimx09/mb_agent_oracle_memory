**Was the flow boundary clear, or did scope creep across?** Clearer after ratification than before. Thread #4's answer on slip-upload ("ใช่ แยกออกมา" — yes, separate flow) settled the one boundary question I'd written tentatively. Thread #5 similarly settled a branch I'd left neutral. The bank-bot breadcrumb addition actively *reinforces* the boundary — "here's where our repo stops" — rather than creeping it outward.

**Are numbered sequence steps actually usable for impl pointers?** Mostly yes. Step 2 still aggregates too much (validate + rotate + gen + persist + SSE publish) — the impl pointers had to be sub-bulleted to five locations. This is a W8-workflow shape issue, not something this retro can fix. Flagged for a future retro on whether Step 2 should have been split into 2a/2b.

**Did Mermaid help or fight on the bank-bot breadcrumb?** Helped, once I realized `Note over X,Y` was the right primitive. Mermaid does not have a first-class notion of "this hop is implemented in a different repo" — the closest equivalents are (a) numbering + `// ext:` label, which I used for steps 4 and 5, or (b) a spanning Note, which I used for 5a. Both are kludges but the `Note over` reads cleanly in GitHub's PR preview and avoids inflating the message count. If W8 were revised to formalize "cross-repo hop" as a distinct marker, I'd adopt it; for now, ad-hoc.

**Should this flow have been split into sub-flows?** No. The 8-numbered + 1-note shape fits comfortably. The three neighbouring flows (deposit-slip-upload-admin-approve, deposit-expiry, withdrawal-queue-dispatch) are correctly out of scope; §Related flows lists them as future W8 passes. Splitting this further would produce wireframe-sized docs.

**On the `ratified revision same-day` pattern.** This retro + 19.49's retro now describe two passes on the same slug within 46 minutes. That's fast — faster than I expected. The reason it worked: the first retro's Honest Feedback already flagged "actor naming feels like it needs a thread" as a likely issue, and the thread answered in under an hour. Two retros on the same slug should probably chain via `related:` links (done above) and not get smashed into one update. The diary + feedback of each pass stays scoped to what that pass did.

**Gap in workflow coverage.** W8 step 11 does not say whether a same-day revision pass writes a *new* retro or amends the prior one. I chose new (P-001 nothing-deleted; each pass is its own evidence chain). Flag for a W8 workflow clarification PR: "each W8 pass — initial or revision — produces its own retro."

**What I will do differently next time.** Nothing material. If anything, next time I'd open the `arra_trace_link` call in the same single-message tool batch as the `arra_trace` creation — I did them sequentially, which was fine but slightly slower. Minor.
