## Honest Feedback

**Was the flow boundary clear, or did scope creep across into a neighboring flow?**

The hardest judgment call of the pass. The `ReconcileFailedPayoutToCompleted` helper is called from **three** distinct places (matcher `finalizePayout`, post-MarkFailed `tryReconcileAfterMarkFailed`, admin `ConfirmPayoutCompleted`). A naive author could have tried to write one flow covering all three — and the `2026-04-16_name-payout-auto-reconcile-three-convergent.md` learning explicitly named all three as convergent paths. The scope-cut decision — "this flow is only the matcher/statement-arrival trigger class; the post-MarkFailed goroutine goes to `payout-request.md` Step 10 / `withdrawal-queue-*` flows; the admin path is already owned by `payout-confirm-completed.md`" — was the load-bearing decision. The slug name `payout-auto-reconcile-from-statement` helped (the *-from-statement suffix scopes to the statement-arrival trigger). Three cross-links in §Related flows make the sibling territory explicit rather than silently carving.

Ratification thread #37 Q2 asks the human to explicitly confirm this cut. If they reject it and want the goroutine absorbed, that's an S4→revision scope change. I think the cut is correct but flagging it for verification is the right W8 discipline.

**Are numbered sequence steps actually usable for impl pointers, or did a step aggregate too much?**

Step 7 ("three ordered writes") aggregates three writes into one self-message, which is more than the per-step-unit-of-commit ideal. The reason: this flow has one atomic session (the `ReconcileFailedPayoutToCompleted` body) sandwiched between two non-session writes (statement CAS, transactions insert) in `finalizePayout`. Breaking them into 3 separate steps would have taken the diagram from 9 to 11 actor-crossings — over the 10-cap. The `Note over` at step 0 handles context; the prose in §Implementation pointers under Step 7 decomposes into six sub-bullets with individual line numbers. That carried the detail without inflating the diagram.

I initially wrote Step 7 at ~55 words (way over the §Mermaid safety rule's "keep short"). Compressed to ~36 words and moved the decomposition into §Implementation pointers — one pass of self-editing caught it before commit. The `deposit-auto-match-from-statement` sibling's Step 7 is ~32 words with similar structure; mine ended up within a few words of that benchmark. Good match.

**Did Mermaid help or fight the diagram? Any step you wanted to express that Mermaid couldn't?**

Mermaid helped. The sequence form matches the temporal causality of this flow (statement arrives, then matcher runs, then atomic commit, then callback) cleanly. No `loop` or `alt` wrapping needed because this is a linear request-response-ish pattern (the goroutine-launch at step 4 is in-message, not a separate loop structure). Following the §Mermaid safety rules was mechanical: hyphen aliases, ASCII-only, no HTML in Notes, no `{...}`, no second `:`, no `;`. Ran `LC_ALL=C grep` to confirm ASCII-only — clean.

That's acceptable — idempotency is a property of the set of runs, not a single run, and prose is the right medium.
