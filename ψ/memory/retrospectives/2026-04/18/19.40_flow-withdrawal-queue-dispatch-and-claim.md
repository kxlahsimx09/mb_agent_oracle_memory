## Honest Feedback

**Was the flow boundary clear, or did scope creep across into a neighboring flow?**

Mostly clear, with one edge. The enqueue boundary is clean — EnqueueWithdrawal is a function call; the flow starts after it returns. The terminal-cascade boundary is blurrier because the cascade produces per-source-specific effects (payout refund, MDR distribution, source status wording) that I *described* here but really *belong* in the per-source flow's §Postconditions. I handled this with §Scope boundary and by being explicit in §Error paths ("for payout/settlement; pullout/direct-transfer have no client wallet") — but a stricter reading might say step 8's cascade detail should be one line ("cascade to source; see per-source flow for effects") rather than the paragraph I wrote.

If the human prefers the stricter cut, it's a few-line revision. My bias was toward explicitness because this is the *first* time the shared cascade is documented in any flow — deferring everything to per-source flows that may not exist yet leaves a hole.

**Are numbered sequence steps actually usable for impl pointers, or did a step aggregate too much?**

Step 3 is the one I wrestled with. It aggregates `findIdleBanks` + `dispatchForBank` atomic lock + per-bank item assignment into one actor-crossing message (Dispatcher → Gateway). Three pointers on one step. Splitting into three steps would have exceeded the 10-step cap and added no intent-level value (the three are always done together in one dispatcher tick; nothing external observes the split). The impl-pointer line for step 3 compensates by listing three distinct `file:line@commit` ranges with separate labels. Workable but at the edge of "too much per step."

Every other step maps cleanly to one impl pointer.

**Did Mermaid help or fight the diagram? Any step you wanted to express that Mermaid couldn't?**

Mermaid did fine. The only thing I wanted and couldn't express cleanly was the *temporal separation* between the dispatcher's assignment (step 3, synchronous within the tick) and the bot's claim (step 4, which may happen seconds or minutes later when the bot next polls). A sequence diagram implies each step follows the previous one immediately; in reality step 3 → step 4 can be a gap of minutes. I noted it in §Purpose but the diagram still *looks* synchronous. Would need a Gantt chart or similar to express this properly; Mermaid supports Gantt but it doesn't combine cleanly with sequenceDiagram.

The `Note over` block for the admin cancel path worked but feels like a hack — cancel is a full actor-crossing message of its own, just one that can't happen after step 4. Putting it in a `Note` under the main sequence means it's visible but not formally part of the numbered-step machinery. Inline would violate the "happy path" narrative. The trade-off feels like the right call but it's not obvious.

**Should this flow have been split into sub-flows?**

Possibly `withdrawal-queue-dispatch` (dispatcher side) vs `withdrawal-queue-claim-and-finalize` (bot side). Arguments for splitting:
- Two actors own each half (Dispatcher + Gateway on one side; BankBot + Gateway on the other).
- The two halves are genuinely asynchronous (see Mermaid note above).
- Each half would be 5-ish steps — under the 10 cap comfortably.

Arguments against:
- The two halves are meaningful only together — neither is a complete operational narrative on its own.
- Splitting doubles the `[RATIFICATION_PENDING]` debt (2 threads instead of 1).
- The bot's claim is trivially pointless without the dispatcher's prior assignment; documenting claim in isolation would start with "preconditions: dispatcher has assigned `system_bank_id`" which is just the other half's output.

I chose not to split. Kept the cut at "dispatch + claim + finalize as one flow." If the human ratification rejects this and asks for a split, it's a half-hour revision to produce two docs.
