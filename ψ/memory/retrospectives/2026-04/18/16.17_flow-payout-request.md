**Key non-obvious facts surfaced (and recorded as docstrings + the §Step-9 learning):**

1. **Wallet debit happens at Phase 1, not on bank confirmation.** This is the structural decision that shapes everything else — the failure path is "refund what was already taken" rather than "credit on success".
2. **System bank balance is decremented by `amount`, not `amount + fee`.** The fee never leaves the gateway — it's split between the client (debited up-front) and partner wallets (credited at MDR distribution). Three-way money flow that is not obvious from any single function.
3. **Double-callback race on auto-reconcile is structural, not accidental.** PR #189 narrowed the trigger but the dispatch shape is unchanged: `processPostCompletion` (sync inside MarkFailed) and `tryReconcileAfterMarkFailed` (goroutine after MarkFailed) can both fire. Documented as Open Question (b).
4. **`waiting_to_review` decouples the bot's observation from the ledger.** No wallet/MDR change happens until admin confirms — so a `waiting_to_review` payout sits in a held state where the customer's money is debited but the bank may or may not have transferred. The §Postconditions section explicitly does NOT cover this branch (it's neither "completed" nor "failed").
5. **`CancelPayout` handler is dead code.** Routes commented out (DRIFT-5 resolved); the function still compiles. Noted in §Error paths to head off future readers wondering why it exists.

## Honest Feedback

**Was the flow boundary clear, or did scope creep across into a neighboring flow?**

Mostly clear. The `waiting_to_review` admin path was the boundary risk — it lives in `controllers/PayoutController.go` (admin-side, JWT) rather than `controllers/PayoutRequestController.go` (client-side, API-Key). I considered carving it into a separate `payout-waiting-to-review-admin-confirm` flow doc the way deposits split slip-upload into its own file, but the asymmetry didn't justify it: deposits have *two distinct entry points* (QR vs slip), while payouts have *one entry point with a branch*. Folded the admin-confirm path into §Error paths instead. If future revisions show the branch growing complex (e.g., dedicated approve/reject UI, multi-step review), it should be split out then — for now keeping it in one doc reads cleaner.

**Are numbered sequence steps actually usable for impl pointers, or did a step aggregate too much?**

Step 9 aggregates the three terminal branches (success / failed / waiting-to-review) into one diagram message. That's fine for the actor-crossing summary (the bot makes one HTTP call regardless of result), but the impl pointers section had to fan out into three sub-bullets to keep each branch addressable. Future W4 queries against a specific status flip will land on the right code path, but a reviewer expecting one impl pointer per step number will be briefly confused. Considered numbering them 9a/9b/9c — rejected because the actor sequence really is "one bot call", not three.

Step 5a (the dispatcher) is marked as internal-to-Gateway and sits as a `Note over` rather than a numbered actor crossing. This is correct per the workflow rule ("No internal helper calls. Only actor-crossing messages.") but feels slightly buried — the dispatcher *behavior* (per-bank locking, idle-bank discovery, immediate-vs-ticker dispatch) is genuinely important to understanding why the bot might or might not see a payout in its next claim window. Considered upgrading Dispatcher to a participant — rejected because it doesn't cross actors, it's gateway-internal logic.

**Did Mermaid help or fight the diagram? Any step you wanted to express that Mermaid couldn't?**

Helped. 4 participants + 10 numbered messages renders cleanly in the GitHub PR preview. The `Note over` syntax for the dispatcher (between Gateway and BankBot) was exactly the right escape hatch for "internal logic that influences the cross-actor sequence". One thing Mermaid can't express well: the **temporal gap** between Phase 1 (sub-second response) and Phase 2 (sub-minute typical bot pickup) and Phase 3 (terminal). I noted it in §Purpose prose instead — Mermaid sequence diagrams compress time uniformly, so a reader can't tell that step 5 to step 6 is "tens of seconds" while step 1 to step 5 is "tens of ms". That's a fundamental Mermaid limitation, not a doc problem.

**Should this flow have been split into sub-flows?**

No. 10 actor-crossing steps is at the upper limit but still single-flow-coherent; the 3 terminal branches (success/fail/waiting) all share Phase 1+2 and only diverge at the last actor crossing. A split would force the reader to chase 3 documents to understand a single payout's fate.

If something *should* eventually split out: the `waiting_to_review` → admin-confirm-completed path. It's effectively a new flow keyed on a *different actor* (Admin) entering at step 9-and-a-half. For now it lives in §Error paths because the admin confirm route is small (~150 LoC); when the admin-side review process grows (notifications, multi-approver, audit comments, etc.), promote it to `payout-waiting-to-review-resolution.md` and link from here.
