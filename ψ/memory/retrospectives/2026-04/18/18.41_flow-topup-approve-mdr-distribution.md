## Honest Feedback

- **Scope boundary was clear but crowded by two adjacent endpoints.** `ProcessTopup` (`POST /topups/:id/process`) and `CancelTopup` (`POST /topups/:id/cancel`) both looked like candidates to fold in. I kept them out — `ProcessTopup` became §Open question (c) because its aliveness is the uncertainty; `CancelTopup` went to §Related flows because it's structurally a separate reversal flow that deserves its own W8 pass. No regret — folding would have pushed the diagram past 10 steps and mixed three different state transitions into one doc. But the decision cost ~5 min of reading before it was confident.
- **Sequence-step granularity borderline at Step 5.** The partner-distribution loop has ~6 sub-operations (status-check → wallet-lookup → `$inc` → change-log → append-to-distributions → continue). The single diagram message says "for each active partner … + wallets_change_logs op="mdr_distribution"" which undersells the complexity. §Implementation pointers Step 5 is a nested bullet list that acts as the real impl contract. Acceptable — the diagram is the summary; the prose disambiguates — but a reviewer relying on the diagram alone will miss the asymmetric skip-vs-abort behaviour that's §Open question (b). That's probably unavoidable for any flow with a per-element loop; noted for future W8 passes.
- **Mermaid helped.** Two actors (Admin + Gateway) with 9 messages (1 request, 6 Gateway self-messages with two `Note over Gateway` blocks bracketing the atomic block, 1 response) is clean enough. Self-messages dominate because this is an admin-only flow — no external hop, no bot. The `Note over Gateway: begin MongoDB session transaction` / `Note over Gateway: commit transaction` pair is doing real work in the diagram: it tells the reader exactly which steps are atomic (3-6) and which are post-commit best-effort (8). Happy with that.
- **No sub-flow split needed.** This flow is ~150 lines, 9 diagram steps, one MDR distribution loop, one post-commit footnote. Well under the "split into sub-flows" threshold. Splitting out the MDR loop would create a flow with 4 steps and no decision logic — worse than useless.
