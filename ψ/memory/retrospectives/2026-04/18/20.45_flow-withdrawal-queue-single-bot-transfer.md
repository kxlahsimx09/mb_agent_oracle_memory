**Key design call — sibling-not-child.** I spent some time weighing "new doc" vs "sub-section in sibling's doc." Explorer agent had recommended sub-section. My call went the other way, for four reasons:
1. User explicitly asked for "อีก diagram" — they wanted a visible, separate artefact.
2. Actor count + step sequence legitimately differ. A sub-section with conditional-sequence prose would be harder to read than two clean diagrams.
3. Bot-writer's future W8 will almost certainly author per-bank-mode on the bank-bot side. Mobiz-side parity helps cross-repo trace chaining (`arra_trace_link` pairs one pg-writer W8 with one bot-writer W8).
4. The stacked-PR pattern exists for exactly this case (sibling flow spec references sibling flow spec still-in-flight). Using it here validates that the pattern is right.

I put design question 1 on thread #13 explicitly, so if the human disagrees I'll merge this doc into the sibling as a mode-branch. Low-cost reversal.

## Honest Feedback

**Was the flow boundary clear, or did scope creep across into a neighboring flow?**

The sibling's already-locked boundary helped — I inherited the "from pending row to terminal" cut and didn't have to re-defend it. Where I could have slipped: the KTB-specific session-keep-alive rotation (app.js:1760-1859 idle loop). I mentioned it once in §Actors and again in §Implementation pointers step 4 but resisted making it a diagram step. It's bot-internal housekeeping that doesn't cross an actor boundary; would have been out-of-scope creep. Stayed out; glad I did.

**Are numbered sequence steps actually usable for impl pointers, or did a step aggregate too much?**

Step 5 is the aggregator this time ("ext-bank-bot login + add recipients + submit + OTP"). Four distinct bot actions collapsed into one line because they're all `BB→BK` and none have gateway-visible intermediate effects. Splitting would exceed the cap and add nothing operators care about. Cleanly aggregable.

Step 7's `/success OR /failed` is also aggregated (single step for two exclusive branches). Acceptable because they go to the same endpoint prefix and differ only in final suffix; reviewer can tell.

**Did Mermaid help or fight the diagram? Any step you wanted to express that Mermaid couldn't?**

Fine. No fights this pass. The `-->>` arrow in step 6 (Bank→BankBotSingle response) is a clean way to show "this edge is a response not a new request" — used sparingly, works.

**Should this flow have been split into sub-flows?**

No. 9 steps is right. Further splitting (e.g., claim-phase as its own flow) would duplicate the sibling and lose the continuity-of-session narrative that makes this flow feel different from dual-control in the first place. Single-session-ness is the *point*.

Conversely, could I have merged this into the sibling as a mode-branch? Yes mechanically, but see design question 1 on thread #13. User said "อีก diagram" (another diagram) — the separate artefact was what they asked for. Making it sub-section would have answered a question they didn't ask.
