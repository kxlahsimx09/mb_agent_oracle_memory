## Honest Feedback

### Was the flow boundary clear, or did scope creep across into a neighboring flow?

Very clear. The endpoint is a single HTTP handler (`CancelPayout` at `controllers/PayoutController.go:913-1079`) with a tight boundary — the step-4 `processing` gate is a hard scope-closer: "the bot has already started, go use `confirm-completed` or `override-status`" is a complete redirect to sibling flows. The four §Related flows siblings did most of the "what's not here" work. The only judgment call was whether to fold `payout-maintenance-cancel` (the `MaintenanceCancelScheduler` path) into this doc or leave it for a future W8 — I deferred per the sibling's precedent (`payout-auto-cancel-pending-timeout.md:148` judged it premature as of `74689ec`; same judgment at `aff85e1`).

Sibling density was both a help and a tax. Helpful: the four existing payout-rail docs (request, auto-cancel-timeout, confirm-completed, withdrawal-queue-*) each needed a §Relationship paragraph, and the skeletons were easy to follow. Tax: the doc is heavy on §Relationship prose — if this rail grows two more admin endpoints, the cross-reference matrix gets unwieldy. A W9 pass in a few weeks will tell whether the pattern scales or collapses.

### Are numbered sequence steps actually usable for impl pointers, or did a step aggregate too much?

Usable. 12 numbered steps with 15 impl pointers (three steps — 4, 5, 7 — split into sub-pointers because the sub-steps live at different line ranges). No step aggregates more than ~15 lines of code; the diagram's `alt` block at step 4 keeps the three-branch queue-state decomposition readable without blowing the 10-crossing cap (4 actor-crossings total, well under the cap).

One small friction: step 5c (short-circuit inside the `alt else` branch) is a `Note over GW` rather than a numbered step, because it describes a zero-match abort within the queue-CAS branch. Mermaid's rendering of notes-inside-alt-blocks is fiddly — I'll find out if it renders cleanly when the PR preview loads. If not, I'll restructure to hoist the abort out of the `alt` and into a numbered step 5e.

### Did Mermaid help or fight the diagram? Any step you wanted to express that Mermaid couldn't?

Mermaid was a help, not a fight, largely because W8 §Mermaid safety rules are now mature (hyphen aliases, ASCII-only messages, no HTML in Notes, no `{...}` structs, no `;` joiners, short self-messages, no second `:` in message tails). I followed the safe template lifted from `topup-approve-mdr-distribution.md` verbatim and didn't have to fight the parser.

One thing Mermaid can't express cleanly: the **"step 4a rejects, step 4c cancels, step 4d skips"** three-way branch is a natural `alt`/`else`/`else` construct — but mermaid's `alt` block rendering numbers the enclosing alt as a single step visually. I worked around by numbering the branches `4a/4c/4d` (as sub-identifiers within the alt condition) rather than renumbering the linear sequence. The impl pointers reference the sub-identifiers. It works but is cosmetically uneven — the diagram's crisp 1→2→3→4 linearity gets a fork at 4, then resumes at 6. A future W8 calibration might standardise "how to number an alt block's branches" — I used a/b/c but some sibling docs use 4.1/4.2/4.3.

### Should this flow have been split into sub-flows?

No. The single doc is the right boundary. If sub-flows had been tempting, they'd have been: (i) the queue-cascade branch (steps 4-5) as its own `payout-queue-cascade` sub-flow, and (ii) the wallet-refund branch (steps 6-8) as its own `payout-wallet-refund` sub-flow. Both ideas fail the "can you name the flow in one sentence" test — neither has a clean actor contract of its own; they're integral to the admin cancel semantics.

What **should** split into sub-flows, eventually: the four sibling admin endpoints on the payout rail (`/cancel`, `/override`, `/confirm-completed`, `/status`) are related enough that a future `payout-admin-lifecycle` *overview* doc linking all four might help operators navigate, but each endpoint has distinct state semantics and deserves its own flow. I'm not queuing that overview yet — let the four individual flows settle first.

### One thing that worked surprisingly well

The four-drift-question pattern with per-item sibling-ruling pairings. By pre-classifying each question and pointing at the ratified sibling ruling, the ratification thread asks for *rulings on my classifications*, not *what do you think about this?* The human can skim, nod, or push back — instead of starting the drift analysis from scratch. This is the fourth flow doc in a row using the four-folded-questions pattern; it has calcified into a template that works. Worth flagging in a W8 calibration: *"for flows whose siblings have ratified drift rulings, pre-classify your own drifts against those rulings; ask the human to rule on your classifications rather than on the raw questions."*

### One thing that might improve next pass

**Open the ratification thread (Step 7) before drafting the doc (Steps 3-6)**, not after. Two benefits: (1) the real thread id is known at draft time, so the `[RATIFICATION_PENDING:<id>]` / `[AWAITING_THREAD:<id>]` markers go in verbatim without a post-hoc Edit pass; (2) the act of writing the thread's "please ratify" prompt forces the author to articulate the three load-bearing framings *first*, which tightens the §Purpose and §Related flows prose when you get to them. The workflow spec doesn't mandate an order; the current default (Steps 3-6 then Step 7) came from the thread-depends-on-the-written-doc intuition, but in practice the thread depends on the *framings* — which you can draft before the doc in the scratchpad. Calibration candidate for workflow-8-flow-map.md §Steps ordering: *"for reverse-engineered flows, Step 7's ratification thread may be opened after Step 2b (root trace) and before Step 3 (drafting), with §Open questions drafted in parallel to §Sequence."*
