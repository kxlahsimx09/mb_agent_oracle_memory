## Lessons Learned
- **Pattern — two orthogonal review lenses beat one**: internal-completeness (next-writer) + vs-running-system (pg-writer) is non-redundant; the second catches silent production-behavior drops the first cannot. Always pair them for "what's missing from a rewrite's requirements."
- **Pattern — relay, don't decide (2a)**: money (settlement fee), security/§9 (step-up posture), and orphan-status (wt-4) are not orchestrator calls. Route to the owning agent / the user. Confident-wrong verdicts cost real corrections.
- **Pattern — carry nuanced specs verbatim**: the user's Q3 answer wasn't "fail-open or fail-closed" — it was "fail-closed default + super-admin-only immediate-effect toggle." Don't collapse a nuanced answer to the menu option you offered; relay the exact spec.
- **Pattern — sequential merge cadence (this user's preference)**: on multi-PR doc campaigns with append-region (glossary/INDEX) conflicts, the user chose merge-each-then-branch-next over batch. Each PR comes out conflict-free. Default to offering it.
- **Pattern — verify deferred lists against HEAD**: a "deferred" dispatch list goes stale; next-writer found 2 of 3 cleanup items already reconciled on main. Don't fabricate edits to match a stale brief.
- **Gotcha — §151 ownership spans sessions**: absence of a worktree from *your* owner records ≠ orphan. Delegate worktree adjudication to brew-ops, which checks per-(oracle,wake-key) across all sessions.
- **Drift — orchestrator Stop-hook is whole-dir, not §151-scoped**: with 3 concurrent orchestrator sessions (wt-20/21/22) it false-blocks each on the others' envelopes. §214 says the sweep-scope and the gate "never disagree" — here they do. Candidate brew-ops fix.

## Honest Feedback
What worked: the directed-inbox protocol scaled cleanly to a 5-campaign, 4-agent, ~30-envelope orchestration without dropping a loop — the envelope-first/archive-second discipline and the per-wake state-grounding are load-bearing. Delegating ops to brew-ops (instead of doing inline `git worktree remove`) was the single best call of the session; it directly prevented killing campaign #231.

What was clumsy: I over-explained the #216 Stop-hook situation to the user three or four times — it became noise. Once characterized as "transient/self-healing, sibling-owned," I should have compressed it to one line per recurrence. I also spent too many internal cycles oscillating on whether to archive wt-21's envelopes; the principle ("don't touch sibling campaigns") was clear from the first occurrence and I should have committed faster. And the recurring false-blocks are a real ergonomic tax that I only *recommended* fixing rather than pushing to actually fix — the brew-ops hook scoping change should probably have been opened as a thread rather than left as a "your call."
