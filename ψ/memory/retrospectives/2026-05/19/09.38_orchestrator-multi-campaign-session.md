## Honest Feedback

**What worked.**
- The directed-inbox dispatch protocol scaled to ~18 threads and 5 oracles
  without losing a campaign (once the bugs were fixed).
- Verify-before-remediate — every "doc-wrong" / "gap" finding was checked before
  an edit. next-impl, next-writer, and next-architect each self-corrected a
  prior claim when it hit the real substrate. That honesty is load-bearing.
- The orchestrator never merged a PR (§9 held throughout) and never touched
  agent work — relayed WHAT+WHY, agents owned HOW.

**What didn't.**
- The inbox-watcher had **four** separate latent defects. The fleet ran for
  weeks with a gc that never retired anything and a loop-closure hook that
  could be defeated by a mis-stamped field. Liveness ≠ correctness — audit the
  *effect*, not just that a daemon is ticking.
- Stacked-PR merge mechanics bit twice: the audit-#168 stack merged into side
  branches because the user merged without retargeting and the branches weren't
  auto-deleted. Recovery cost a whole thread (#169). The orchestrator should
  state the retarget requirement *and offer to do it* up front, every time.
- Orchestrator status reporting drifted from reality during #170 — a deferred
  dispatch is not "in flight." Trust the watcher log's ALERT lines.

**For next time.** When dispatching a `--resume`-eligible long-lived session,
re-state the PR/branch state the agent may have stale memory of. When handing a
stacked-PR merge plan, retarget proactively. Watch the watcher log for
`DEFERRED … past T2` ALERTs as a first-class health signal.
